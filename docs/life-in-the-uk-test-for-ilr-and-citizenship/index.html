Life in the UK Test for ILR and British citizenship

Most adults who apply for settlement or British citizenship must pass the test. Here is who needs it and who does not.

Who needs to pass

If you are applying for indefinite leave to remain (ILR, also called settlement) or for British citizenship by naturalisation, you usually need to pass the Life in the UK Test and include your pass notification letter reference in your application.

Who may not need to take it

  • People under 18 and people aged 65 or over.
  • Some people with a long-term physical or mental condition. You may need to provide evidence.

Exemptions and routes differ, so confirm yours on GOV.UK or with a regulated immigration adviser.

One test, two uses

It is the same test for ILR and for citizenship. If you pass for one, the pass notification letter can usually be used for the other, because it does not expire. Keep the letter safe.

What else you may need

The Life in the UK Test is only one requirement. Applicants also usually need to show their level of English, for example at B1 on the CEFR scale, and meet residence and other requirements for their route. Rules change, so check the current guidance on GOV.UK before applying.

Prepare for it

Use the study guide and the free practice questions, then take mock tests until you score 21 or more out of 24.

This page is general information, not legal advice. For your own case, use GOV.UK or an adviser regulated in the UK.

Quick questions

Do I need the Life in the UK Test for ILR?

Usually yes, unless you are exempt, for example because of your age or a long-term condition.

Is the test the same for citizenship and settlement?

Yes. It is the same test.

Does the Life in the UK Test pass expire?

No. Keep your pass notification letter safe.
