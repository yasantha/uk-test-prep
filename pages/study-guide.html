The test format

The Life in the UK Test is a computer-based test taken at an approved test centre. Most people applying for settlement (indefinite leave to remain) or British citizenship need to pass it.

24multiple-choice questions
45 minto answer them all
18correct answers to pass (75%)

Each question has either one correct answer or asks you to choose two, and some are true or false statements. The questions are based on the official handbook, Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents (3rd edition). You do not need to know anything that is not in the handbook.

What the handbook covers

The handbook has five chapters. History is the longest, so expect it to take the most study time.

  1. 1The values and principles of the UKDemocracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, tolerance and taking part in community life. Short and worth reading first, because the rest of the book builds on it.
  2. 2What is the UK?The four countries, their capitals, the difference between Great Britain and the United Kingdom, and the Crown dependencies.
  3. 3A long and illustrious historyFrom Stone Age Britain and the Romans to the Tudors, the Industrial Revolution, two world wars and Britain since 1945. Lots of names and dates.
  4. 4A modern, thriving societyReligion, festivals and traditions, sport, music, literature, film and famous places to visit.
  5. 5The UK government, the law and your roleThe monarchy, Parliament, elections, the courts, your rights and responsibilities, and how to take part in your community.

A 4-week study plan

Twenty to thirty minutes a day is enough for most people. Short, regular sessions work better than a few long ones.

  1. Week 1: the foundations. Read chapters 1 and 2, then start chapter 3 up to the end of the Middle Ages. Answer practice questions after each section.
  2. Week 2: history. Finish chapter 3, from the Tudors to the present day. Make a short list of the dates and people you keep forgetting.
  3. Week 3: society and government. Study chapters 4 and 5. Keep reviewing the history questions you got wrong.
  4. Week 4: mock tests. Take a full 24-question mock every day or two. Spend the rest of your time on your weakest chapter. Aim to score 21 or more out of 24 in your last few mocks before you book or sit the test.

The app follows this pattern for you: it builds a daily plan from the sections you find hardest and brings back the questions you missed until you know them.

How to study well

  • Learn from wrong answers. When you get a question wrong, read the explanation and the note for that section before moving on.
  • Group the dates. Learn history as a timeline: who came before whom and what changed. It is easier than memorising single dates.
  • Watch the wording. Look out for words like "not" and questions that ask you to pick two answers.
  • Practise under time. 45 minutes for 24 questions is plenty, but timed mocks help you stay calm on the day.
  • Read aloud. Hearing questions and notes helps if English is not your first language. The app can read them to you.

Booking and test day

Book your test online on the official GOV.UK Life in the UK Test page. It explains the current fee, which identification and documents to bring, the rules on the day and what to do if you do not pass. Check it before you book, because the rules can change.

On the day, arrive early, bring exactly what GOV.UK asks for and read each question carefully. You will get your result before you leave the test centre.
